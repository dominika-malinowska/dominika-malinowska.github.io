A successful PhD defence

Created on November 18, 2025

2025

PhDone! 🎓

I am beyond happy to share that this week I successfully passed my PhD viva, so pending some very minor corrections and administrative work, I’m a doctor! 🥳

Huge thanks to my examiners, Oriol Monserrat and Alan Hunter, for making the viva a truly enjoyable experience. It was great to talk about my research with you.

When I started this PhD, I knew very little about SAR, couldn’t really code, and had almost no idea about Machine Learning. I finished with a thesis that includes:

  • An ML-based predictive model for PS published in IEEE JSTARS - my first proper ML project that turned out to be just the beginning;
  • A global analysis of MT-InSAR applicability for bridges, published in Nature Communications (Editors’ Highlights selection), which advanced my coding optimisation and data visualisation skills;
  • A third paper on the potential social impact of SAR-based monitoring, currently under review, that taught me lots about data science and statistics.

This thesis was written while navigating a global pandemic, experiencing the transition to motherhood (see my son’s art on my desk, which was a sweet reminder of him during my viva ☺️), and the stress of a job search and an international move. There were moments I wanted to give up, and I am proud of myself for persevering, which I only managed thanks to the amazing support I had.

I couldn’t have achieved this without the continuous support of my supervisory team. Giorgia Giardina, thank you for being my mentor and for teaching me invaluable skills in research and communication, as well as sharing your insights on feminism 😉. You have contributed significantly not only to my growth as a researcher but also as a person, and I will be forever grateful for the impact you have had on me. Cormac Reale, Chris Blenkinsopp, and Kevin Briggs, thank you for all our discussions, your insightful comments, and for always ensuring that my work maintained its research focus without deviating too far into practicalities. Pietro Milillo, thank you for sharing your knowledge about InSAR and the research guidance. I could not have dreamed of a better supervisory team, thank you!

I would also like to thank all the colleagues I have met at both the University of Bath and Delft University of Technology, as well as the external collaborators I have had the privilege to work with along the way. I loved hearing about your research, and our discussions consistently stimulated new ideas and helped me overcome obstacles.

And a special shout-out to my husband, Mateusz Malinowski, for supporting me through all my moments of doubt, always cheering me up, and believing in me ❤️

I am looking forward to applying the skills gained during my PhD journey in my professional career beyond academia! 🚀
