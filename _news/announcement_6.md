Can we make bridge maintenance more "fair"? 🛠️

---

This is the question we were trying to answer in the latest (and last!) of my PhD papers. After analysing over 22,000 bridges in California, we showed that conventional approaches for bridge maintenance prioritisation often overlook vulnerable communities and therefore could perpetuate social inequality.

To perform this analysis, we developed a new multi-dimensional Bridge Vulnerability Index (BVI) that incorporates subsidence susceptibility, economic resilience, inspection burden, and the availability of MT-InSAR-based monitoring. 

One of the most interesting findings for me was that, in California, bridges in the most vulnerable communities are actually among those most suitable for MT-InSAR monitoring. Therefore, MT-InSAR could (and should!) be used in such communities to support more equitable infrastructure management.

Read the full (Open Access) paper here: [Open Access](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212420926001275?via%3Dihub)

This work is the culmination of my PhD journey, building on the research I have done for the two previous papers:

- How to predict MT-InSAR availability in 📄 *Coherence-based prediction of multi-temporal InSAR measurement availability for infrastructure monitoring*: [Open Access](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10646490).
- How to use those predictions to assess a bridge's potential for monitoring in 📄 *Global geo-hazard risk assessment of long-span bridges enhanced with InSAR availability*: [Open Access](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-64260-x).

One of my private goals for my PhD research was to work on something that could actually be useful and bring a positive change to the world. I'm proud that this last paper is adding a small building block towards more socially equal infrastructure maintenance.
